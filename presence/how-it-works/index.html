How does remote presence advisory actually work?

Entirely remotely, in writing and on video. You send photographs and your next ninety days of commitments. You get back a written assessment, a specific plan, and sourcing detailed enough to execute locally yourself. No shopping trips, no fittings.

The remote constraint is deliberate rather than a limitation. It moves the cost from logistics into judgement, and it means the output is a document you keep and re-read rather than an afternoon you half-remember.

What you send

Four things, and the specification for each arrives once an engagement is confirmed.

Photographs

Taken to a short written spec — front, both profiles and back, against a plain wall, in even daylight, in fitted clothing, with the camera at chest height on a timer rather than held by someone. Plus one photograph of yourself in what you would have worn to your next important meeting, unedited and not staged. That last one is the most informative single item in the whole process.

Measurements

Nine measurements taken to written instructions with a soft tape. It takes about ten minutes with someone helping, or fifteen alone. These exist so that sourcing advice is specific rather than generic — the gap between your shoulder and chest measurement, for instance, decides which jacket cuts will ever fit you off the peg and which will not.

An inventory

Photographs of what you already own and actually wear, laid flat. Not the whole wardrobe — the rotation. Most first passes find that the answer is already partly in the room, and that the real problem is which third of it you reach for.

Your next ninety days

Dates, cities, and what each commitment is: partner meetings, a demo day, a recorded interview, a board meeting, a conference. This is the part founders under-supply and it is the part that determines everything. An abstract improvement to how you dress is worth far less than being correctly calibrated for the six situations actually in front of you.

What comes back

A written document, in plain language, structured the same way every time:

  1. The current read. What you are being read as now, stated directly. This is the part people find uncomfortable and it is the part they are paying for.
  2. The four areas, scored. In person, on camera, consistency across public images, and situational fit — the same four as the diagnostic, assessed properly rather than self-reported.
  3. Ranked fixes. Ordered by return on effort, not by cost. Alterations and subtraction almost always sit above purchases.
  4. Sourcing. Specific items, price bands, what to look for and what disqualifies a candidate, written so you can walk into a shop in your own city and decide in five minutes.
  5. Situation notes. A short entry for each commitment on your ninety-day list, saying what to wear and why.

Then a video call to go through it, and a revision afterwards if the plan needs to change once you have reacted to it.

What this is not: no confidence coaching, no public-speaking training, no social-media branding, no ghostwriting, no in-person shopping. If the problem is what you say rather than how you are read before you say it, this is the wrong service and I will tell you that rather than sell you an engagement.

Questions

Is any of this in person?

No. Every engagement is remote, in writing and on video. There are no shopping trips, no in-person fittings and no studio days. Sourcing is specified precisely enough that you execute it locally yourself, which is faster for you and keeps the cost in the advice rather than in travel.

What do you actually need from me?

Photographs taken to a short specification I send you, your measurements taken to written instructions, a list of what you already own that you wear regularly, and your next ninety days of commitments with dates and cities. The commitments matter most — they decide priority.

How long does it take?

A strategy session is booked within about a week and the written follow-up arrives within three working days of the call. An audit is about a week from receiving your material. A Build runs two to three weeks, most of which is you sourcing and altering at your own pace.

Do you buy anything for me?

No. You are given specific items with names, price bands and what to look for, and you buy them yourself wherever you are. This avoids markup, avoids international shipping and returns, and means you end up understanding your own wardrobe rather than owning a delivered one.

What if I disagree with the advice?

Say so and we work out whether it is a calibration problem or a comfort problem, because they need different answers. Advice you will not act on has no value, and a plan you will not wear is worse than no plan. Nothing here is prescriptive for its own sake.

How do I pay, and when?

Payment links will be added shortly — the account is being set up in Cape Town in the coming week. Until then, an enquiry is answered with an invoice and bank details, and nothing is scheduled until the first payment clears.
